Mind of Jezowa Woda

Jeżowa Woda, Poland
February–May 2023
Academic project

A place that remembers its visitors.

A mountain lodge and observation deck on Jeżowa Woda, Poland. The proposal connects a journey above the trees with an exchange of photographs, memories and messages to the future.

See the proposal in its landscape.

The views show the lodge and tower across the forest in different seasons. The film follows the proposal from above.

Arrive, gather and rest

From arrival to the lobby, restaurant and spa, these interior studies describe everyday life within the proposed lodge.

Start with the mountain.

The site study brings together access, orientation and tree heights. These conditions shape two connected experiences: a sheltered base close to the landscape, and a viewing room that rises beyond the canopy.

More images 02

Make the ascent part of the architecture.

A lodge, communication shaft and observation room form a vertical sequence. The looping silhouette refers to continuity and memory, while the elevation relates each part to the slope and the surrounding trees.

More images 01

A visit becomes a memory.

The proposed visitor journey starts with an NFC touchpoint and a photograph shared to MJW. A unique code links that contribution to a print at the viewing deck. Souvenir coins and a Future Post Office extend the experience into a message to a future self.

A school that belongs to life on the water.

An academic proposal for a floating school in Makoko, Lagos. Learning spaces, boat access and community uses are brought together within a lightweight structural and water-management concept.

Learning, reading and meeting on the water

Bring the school closer to daily life

A school with a public life.

THE DREAM OF SHUNDE

Shunde, Guangdong, China
August–October 2024
Academic project

Let the festival shape everyday life.

An urban renewal proposal for Ronggui in Shunde. Dragon Boat Festival traditions guide the reuse of existing buildings and a system of removable public-space modules that gather for events and disperse for everyday use.

Read the city through its rituals.

Dragon boat activities link the canal, neighbourhood and local cultural life. The project treats the festival as a framework for reconnecting these places, with shared resources that remain useful after the event.

Read the canal and the neighbourhood

The site drawings locate the festival routes and participants, then compare factories, open spaces, waterfronts, housing and arcade streets.

Decide what the old town can become.

Site and fabric analysis lead to an Analytic Hierarchy Process assessment of renewal potential. Social and cultural value, building quality, economic considerations, space use and environmental factors inform a hierarchy of retention, conversion and demolition.

One neighbourhood. Two rhythms.

Removable modules connect the scale of a public event with that of a neighbourhood service. Cultural rooms, a dragon boat club and community canteens retain everyday uses while accommodating additional festival activities.

Modules disperse among neighbourhood facilities. Cultural rooms support local services, the club supports training, and canteens provide shared meals.

Four moments along the festival route

Dragon-boat launch, Cai Qing, watching the races and shared meals organise the festival. Each drawing relates the activity to a place, a period of use and a number of removable modules.

After the festival, return to the neighbourhood

The modules disperse among a cultural parlour, a dragon boat club, a landscape gazebo and community canteens. The drawings describe the everyday uses and their relationship to festival activities.

From removable modules to timber joints

Four original connection studies show how the timber members meet within the module system.

AT A GLANCE

Assess before changing

The renewal assessment considers cultural value, building condition and use before proposing retention, conversion or demolition.

Gather and disperse

Removable modules gather for dragon boat events, then disperse among neighbourhood facilities for everyday use.

Harmonia

Guangdong, China
August–October 2023
Academic project

Reconnect housing, shared services and nature.

An academic urban design exploration in Guangdong. Harmonia uses an adaptable cellular system to investigate how greenery, public services and connections could support a more balanced everyday environment.

Identify the missing relationships.

The site research in Zhuhai’s Jinwan District examines housing, infrastructure and green space together. The project focuses on the mismatch between residential development and the daily environments available to residents.

Translate needs into spatial priorities.

Importance–Performance Analysis compares the importance of environmental qualities with satisfaction. Alongside this assessment, the design concept explores belonging, attachment and security as questions for the spatial proposal.

From environmental needs to urban intervention

The concept connects environmental concerns to spatial responses. Two approaches to intervention are compared before the proposal brings shared spaces and greenery back into the residential fabric.

Grow a connected system from a cell.

The biological analogy progresses from cell proliferation and differentiation to tissue and organ formation. Variants of the polyhedral unit are shown with enclosed space or planting; grouping them creates terraces and larger shared structures. These drawings test a spatial system rather than a constructed building method.

Bring the system back to the neighbourhood.

Shared activities connect residents to community services. The feedback diagram studies how the proposed system could adjust, while grouped cells and shared routes establish its spatial connections.

Between the towers, a shared landscape

The street-level view tests how planted routes and shared spaces could connect to the existing neighbourhood

AT A GLANCE

Compare needs with experience

Importance–Performance Analysis compares environmental priorities with reported satisfaction to guide the proposal.

Combine occupied and planted cells

Different units form shared terraces and routes between existing buildings. The drawings explore spatial organisation, not a tested construction system.

About

Lorenzo LinArchitecture and urban design

I study the relationship between public space and everyday life: how a school serves its neighbourhood, how a waterfront accommodates both festivals and ordinary days, and how a building changes our experience of a landscape. These questions guide my work across architecture and urban design.

I am a graduate of the Master of Urban Design programme at UCL The Bartlett. L²ATELIER brings together my academic projects and studies in architecture and urban design.

L²L² starts with two L-shaped lines. Their overlap suggests a space: from a line to a place, from a building to its surroundings.
